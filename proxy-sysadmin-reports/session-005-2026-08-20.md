# Session 5 — Netdata `web_log` Alarm Flapping: Diagnosis & Fix

**Date:** 2026-08-20
**Server:** `root@proxy` (`46.250.234.153`, internal `10.0.0.2`)
**OS:** Ubuntu 24.04.3 LTS
**Goal:** `telegram-alerts` ledger pull (backfilled Aug 10 → Aug 20) showed netdata's `web_log_1m_*` alarms flapping constantly on `proxy`. Diagnose and fix.

---

## Root Cause

Netdata's stock `health.d/web_log.conf` alarms (`web_log_1m_successful`, `_redirects`, `_bad_requests`, `_internal_errors`) compute a **1-minute** ratio of nginx response-code buckets, guarded to only evaluate when `>120` requests occurred in that minute. `proxy` fronts a catch-all vhost that continuously absorbs internet background noise — vulnerability-scanner probes (`wp_filemanager.php`, random webshell-named `.php` paths, no matching `Host` header → 301/404), Uptime-Kuma health-check polling (`GET /` across ~9-15 monitors/min, some legitimately 302/307), and similar. During any single minute where this noise is a larger share of traffic than usual, the 1-minute ratio swings hard enough to cross the stock thresholds (85%/95% success, >20%/1% redirects) and trip CRITICAL/WARNING — then clears a minute or two later. This isn't a real incident: OpenObserve's dedicated `Nginx_5xx_Spike` alert (actual-error-focused, better tuned) never fired in the same window.

Pulled ledger evidence: **764 CRITICAL transitions on `web_log_1m_successful` alone in the prior 4 days** (~1 every 7-8 min), confirmed live at diagnosis time (51.98% successful, 29.97% redirects, both past threshold for that minute) while a random 100-line sample of the access log showed a healthy 96% success rate — confirming the badness is concentrated in short bursts, not sustained.

A separate, compounding issue: `proxy` streams its metrics to `monitor` as a netdata parent/child pair (`stream.conf`: `destination = 10.0.0.3:19999`). `monitor`'s stream config had `health enabled by default = auto`, so `monitor` was **independently re-evaluating the same `web_log` alarms on the streamed data** and firing its own near-duplicate Telegram notification for every flap — confirmed by near-identical CRITICAL counts on both hosts (997 vs 996) for the same alarm over the same window. Fixed on `monitor`'s side — see [monitor-sysadmin-reports/session-010](../monitor-sysadmin-reports/session-010-2026-08-20.md).

## Config Change

`/etc/netdata/health.d/web_log.conf` (new local override; stock `/usr/lib/netdata/conf.d/health.d/web_log.conf` untouched):

```diff
 template: web_log_1m_requests
-   lookup: sum -1m unaligned
+   lookup: sum -5m unaligned

 template: web_log_1m_successful
-   lookup: sum -1m unaligned of success
-    warn: ($web_log_1m_requests > 120) ? ...
-    crit: ($web_log_1m_requests > 120) ? ...
+   lookup: sum -5m unaligned of success
+    warn: ($web_log_1m_requests > 600) ? ...
+    crit: ($web_log_1m_requests > 600) ? ...

 template: web_log_1m_redirects / web_log_1m_bad_requests / web_log_1m_internal_errors
   (same pattern: -1m → -5m, guard 120 → 600, percentage thresholds unchanged)
```

Rationale: widening the averaging window from 1 to 5 minutes lets a short scanner burst dilute against surrounding clean traffic instead of tripping the alarm outright, while a genuinely sustained problem (5 minutes of real bad ratio) still alarms at the same severity. Raising the volume guard proportionally (120→600, same ~2 req/s bar) keeps the "enough data" semantics consistent with the new window — and turned out to do useful extra work: it now also suppresses alerting during genuinely low-traffic overnight periods where a handful of monitoring/bot requests would otherwise dominate the ratio.

Chose *tune, don't disable* over disabling the 4 alarms outright, since OpenObserve's `Nginx_5xx_Spike` only covers 5xx and these still add coverage for a real sustained redirect/4xx anomaly (e.g. a broken deploy).

## Verification

`curl localhost:19999/api/v1/alarms?all=true` post-fix shows all 4 alarms sourced from the new override file with a 300s lookup window; live-watched for ~5 minutes and confirmed the alarm state transitioned from CRITICAL (riding out an old burst still inside the window) to stable CLEAR as the window slid into current low-traffic data. Full validation needs a daytime cycle where real traffic and bot noise mix more than the overnight lull this was verified against — recheck the `telegram-alerts` ledger flap count in a day or two.
