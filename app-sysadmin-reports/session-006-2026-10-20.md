# Session 6 — Fleet SSH mesh: `devops@app` → cluster roots, host aliases, fail2ban allow-lists

**Date:** 2026-10-20
**Server:** `devops@app` / `root@app` (154.26.129.104 / 10.0.0.5), plus `proxy`, `db`, `erp`, `monitor`, `backup`, `dev` as targets (fleet-wide, filed here because `devops@app` is the actor that was blocked)
**OS:** Ubuntu 24.04.4 (app)

**Summary:** The request was to let every server SSH to every other (numa excluded: reachable from the Mac only). The survey showed root-to-root was **already a full mesh**: all 30 pairs among proxy, db, erp, monitor, backup and app worked over `10.0.0.x`, and all six reach `root@dev` over its public IP. They share one RSA key (`root@prod`, `SHA256:nnFQ+eCe…`) that sits in every `/root/.ssh/id_rsa` and in every `authorized_keys`. The real gaps were the deploy accounts: `devops@app` (the user that builds and deploys) could reach nothing but `deploy@dev`, and `root@dev` / `deploy@dev` had no outbound access. This session authorized `devops@app` on the five cluster roots (pinned `from="10.0.0.5"`), added short-name SSH aliases on all hosts, and widened fail2ban `ignoreip` on app and dev. dev outbound (D2) was declined by the user (dev stays receive-only). A later read-only check of numa found no fleet key on it, and the static-deploy skill and profiles were switched to the direct `devops@app` → `root@proxy` path.
