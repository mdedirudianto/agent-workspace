# Session 34 — Download failure analysis: the progressive player client collapsed and the datacenter proxy plan ran out

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Servers:** read-only on `root@numa` (10.0.0.7), `root@db` (10.0.0.1), `devops@app` (154.26.129.104), `root@proxy` (46.250.234.153)
**Trigger:** user reports "errors on download lately"
**Scope:** **analysis only — nothing was changed.** No env edits, no restarts, no deploys. Every command was a read, a query, or a throwaway `yt-dlp`/`curl` probe.
**Result:** Four independent causes identified and separated, three of them confirmed by live A/B probes on production hosts. The headline: **`YTDLP_PROGRESSIVE_PLAYER_CLIENT=web_safari` — the fix shipped in session-033 — has stopped working entirely (0/14 on failing videos, 1/5 on a control set), and `mweb` replaces it cleanly.** Separately, the **datacenter proxy plan hit its 250 GB cap on 2026-08-31** and that whole tier is dead, leaving 10 residential proxies carrying every download at ~165 KB/s.

---

## Summary

There is no single "download error". There are **four separate faults** stacked on top of each other, and they are worth keeping apart because they have different fixes, different urgencies, and different owners:

| # | Fault | Evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RC1** | **`web_safari` progressive client no longer resolves itag 18.** Session-033 measured it at 8/8. Today it is **0/14** on recently-failed videos and **1/5** on a control set of videos that *succeeded* three days ago. | Live A/B on `numa`, §3.1 | **P0** — drives the 47–66% `muxed` failure rate |
| **RC2** | **Datacenter proxy plan exhausted** — 252 GB used of a 250 GB cap (100.7%). Tier has served **zero** requests since 2026-08-31 21:37. | `proxy_plan_usage`, `proxies.last_ok_at`, §3.2 | **P0** — removes 250 of 260 proxies from the pool |
| **RC3** | **All download traffic now rides 10 residential proxies at ~165 KB/s** (vs 8.5 MB/s direct — **50x slower**). Users give up mid-download. | Throughput probe, `download_events.status='aborted'`, §3.3 | **P1** — aborts are the *largest* non-success bucket (38% of today's events) |
| **RC4** | **`curl_cffi` is not installed on either API node**, so yt-dlp has **no impersonation target at all** and presents a scriptable TLS fingerprint to YouTube. 11,633 `BotChallengeError` in the recent log window. | `yt-dlp --list-impersonate-targets`, §3.4 | **P1** — the single largest error type in the logs |

**Ruled out** (each of these was a plausible suspect and each is *not* the cause): yt-dlp is out of date (it is on `2026.08.19`, the **latest** PyPI release), the API is unhealthy (both nodes `status=ok`, db+redis green), the web app is broken (200 in 0.42 s), disk pressure (31% / 37%), and the POT provider being down (it is up and actively minting tokens).

The default now yields pot-less `WEB_EMBEDDED_PLAYER` / `VISIONOS` URLs, and those are precisely the URLs googlevideo refuses. So a resolution failure quietly becomes a media-403 one layer down. That is why the logs are full of 403s while the actual defect is a *resolver* regression.

### 3.2 RC2 — the datacenter proxy plan is exhausted (P0)

```
 day        | kind        | used   | limit  | pct    | projected
 2026-09-01 | datacenter  | 252 GB | 250 GB | 100.7  | 373 GB
 2026-09-01 | residential | 161 GB | 250 GB |  64.4  | 239 GB
```

And the pool is functionally dead:

```
 kind        | active | ok_last_6h | fail_last_6h | newest_ok
 datacenter  |    250 |          0 |            0 | 2026-08-31 21:37:55
 residential |     10 |         10 |            4 | 2026-09-01 17:16:09
```

All 250 datacenter proxies are still `status=active` in the database — nothing marked them down, because they are not *failing*, they are simply never selected once Webshare starts answering 402 and the quota backoff parks the tier. `proxy_daily_stats` for today has **no datacenter row at all**.

The interaction with session-033's tier-order flip is what makes this bite. `PROXY_DOWNLOAD_KIND_ORDER=datacenter,residential,direct` puts the *exhausted* tier first; it yields immediately, and everything lands on residential.

**Residential is next.** It went 142 → 145 → 157 → 161 GB across 08-29 → 09-01, i.e. ~12 GB/day since datacenter died. At that rate it hits its own 250 GB cap in **roughly 7 days (~2026-09-08)**. When it does, downloads fall to `direct`, which §3.5 shows is the worst tier. This is a dated deadline, not a vague risk.

### 3.3 RC3 — 10 residential proxies at 165 KB/s, and that is why users abort (P1)

Same URL, 3 MB range request, from `numa`:

```
residential_proxy : http=206  speed=  168,784 B/s  time=18.6s
direct            : http=206  speed=8,551,801 B/s  time= 0.4s
```

**~50x slower.** A 50 MB video takes about 5 minutes through the residential pool. Today's 179 aborts against 216 successes are the direct consequence — the aborts are not users changing their minds, they are users outlasted by the transfer. This also explains why `audio` (107 aborts) and `video` (37 aborts) abort heavily while barely *failing*: those requests work, they are just unbearably slow.

Ten proxies serving the entire product is also a concentration risk in its own right: it is a small enough set for YouTube to fingerprint, which feeds RC4.

### 3.4 RC4 — no TLS impersonation available (P1)

On **both** API nodes:

```
$ yt-dlp --list-impersonate-targets
Client    OS   Source
Tor       -    curl_cffi>=0.11 (unavailable)
Edge      -    curl_cffi      (unavailable)
Firefox   -    curl_cffi>=0.10 (unavailable)
Safari    -    curl_cffi      (unavailable)
Chrome    -    curl_cffi      (unavailable)
```

`/opt/ytdlp-venv` contains only `yt-dlp 2026.8.19` and `yt-dlp-ejs 0.8.0`. **No `curl_cffi`, no `Brotli`.** A live caption fetch surfaces it as a warning in plain text:

> `WARNING: The extractor specified to use impersonation for this download, but no impersonate target is available.`

Without `curl_cffi`, every request yt-dlp makes carries Python's TLS/JA3 fingerprint. YouTube fingerprints on exactly this. With 11,633 `BotChallengeError` and 18,002 "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot" in the window, this is the most likely single lever on the bot-challenge volume — and it is a one-package install, not a code change.

Also noted: `db` runs **Python 3.10**, which yt-dlp now prints a deprecation warning for ("Please update to Python 3.11 or above"). And `YTDLP_COOKIES_FILE` is unset, so there is no authenticated session to fall back on either.

### 3.5 A correction to session-033: the exit IP *does* matter now

Session-033 concluded the media-403 "reproduces at the same rate on the server's own IP, on datacenter proxies, on residential proxies, and from an unrelated home residential line", and renamed `IP_LOCK_FAILED` → `MEDIA_FETCH_403` on that basis. **That conclusion no longer holds.** Same URL, same moment, two exits:

| video | direct (numa IP) | residential proxy |
|---|---|---|
| 1cgOsexGFlw | **403** | 206 |
| 3pCJFuYINUc | **403** | 206 |
| 5dv6pOGeuGs | 403 | 403 |

And the aggregate agrees — `direct` has been the worst tier every single day, all the way through the 08-18→08-23 spike and out the other side: 51–71% download failure while datacenter sat at 12–45% and residential at 7–50%.

The rename to `MEDIA_FETCH_403` was still correct — the failure is a media-fetch refusal, and naming it after a disproven mechanism was the right thing to undo. But **"exit IP is irrelevant" should not be carried forward as settled**; on today's evidence YouTube is penalising the server's own address. This matters because it is the argument against "just fall back to direct when the proxy plans run out".

## 4. Diagnosis in one paragraph

The `muxed` failure rate is a **resolver regression**: `web_safari`, which session-033 chose and verified at 8/8, now fails to resolve itag 18 for 80–100% of videos, and the deliberate fallback to yt-dlp's default client produces pot-less URLs that googlevideo refuses. That is RC1 and it is the thing that is actually "broken lately". Underneath it, the **capacity floor gave way on 2026-08-31** when the datacenter plan hit 100.7% of its 250 GB cap and dropped out, leaving 10 residential proxies to carry everything at 165 KB/s — which converts into the 38% abort rate, and which will repeat on residential around 2026-09-08. Sitting across both is a **missing `curl_cffi`**, which denies yt-dlp any TLS impersonation and is the most plausible driver of 11,633 bot challenges. The 08-18→08-23 spike to 67% was a *separate*, proxy-layer event that resolved on its own and is not the current fault.

## 5. Recommended plan

Ordered by (impact ÷ risk). **Nothing below has been applied.** Steps 1–3 are env/package only — no code, no deploy, no migration — and each is independently revertible.

### Step 1 — `YTDLP_PROGRESSIVE_PLAYER_CLIENT`: `web_safari` → `mweb` (P0, env-only)

The highest-value change in this report and the cheapest. On both API nodes: back up `.env`, change the one line, `pm2 restart ytgrab-api`, restart `numa` first and confirm before touching `db` so the LB keeps serving.

- **Expect:** `muxed` failures fall from ~57% toward ~10–20%.
- **Measure:** `muxed` fail % per hour for 2 hours against today's 57.1% baseline.
- **Revert:** one line + restart.
- **Caveat worth stating plainly:** `mweb` was 10–11/14 and 5/5-resolve in my probes, not 14/14. It is a large improvement, not a cure. Some videos will still fail, and after Step 1 lands, the residue should be re-sampled rather than assumed to be the same population.
- **Follow-on to consider:** make the progressive client a *prioritised list* (`mweb,web_safari,android`) tried in order rather than one value with a silent fallback to yt-dlp's default. Today a resolve failure degrades into a pot-less URL and shows up as a 403 two layers away, which is what made this regression hard to see. **This is a code change, not env — Phase-gated, separate PR.**

### Step 2 — install `curl_cffi` on both API nodes (P1, package-only)

`/opt/ytdlp-venv/bin/pip install "curl_cffi>=0.11" brotli`, then confirm `yt-dlp --list-impersonate-targets` lists Chrome/Firefox/Safari as available.

- **Expect:** a fall in `BotChallengeError` volume; possibly a fall in `direct`-tier 403s too.
- **Measure:** `BotChallengeError`/hour before vs after (currently 2–84/hour on `numa`).
- **Risk:** low, but it is a new native dependency in the venv — do `numa` first, watch for 30 minutes, then `db`.
- **Note:** `db` is on Python 3.10 and yt-dlp already warns it is deprecated. Check the wheel installs cleanly there; a Python upgrade on `db` is a bigger job and should be its own session.

### Step 3 — decide the proxy-capacity question (P0, commercial + env)

This one needs a business decision, not just a config change. Three options, and they are not mutually exclusive:

| Option | Effect | Cost | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3a. Top up / upgrade the Webshare datacenter plan** | Restores 250 proxies; throughput back to 42+ KB/s tier with real breadth | $ | Fastest path back to normal. Residential still hits its own cap ~09-08 regardless |
| **3b. Buy more residential bandwidth** | Raises the ~09-08 ceiling | $$ | Residential is 93–165 KB/s — relieves failures, not aborts |
| **3c. Reorder to `residential,datacenter,direct`** | Stops wasting the first attempt on a tier that is guaranteed to yield | free | **Do this regardless of 3a/3b** — with datacenter at 402 the current order burns an attempt every download. Revert `.env.bak.tierorder.20260817-234602` is *not* the right revert here; `direct`-first is worse (§3.5) |

My recommendation: **3c immediately** (free, strictly better while datacenter is capped), and **3a** as the real fix. Hold **3b** until Step 1 lands, because fixing RC1 should cut total bytes — a download that fails at 57% is still *spending* proxy bandwidth on its failed attempts.

**Set a calendar reminder for ~2026-09-06** to re-check residential burn against the 250 GB cap. This is the second time a plan cap has taken the service down without warning; the projection column in `proxy_plan_usage` disagreed with the actual delta both times, so trust the day-over-day delta.

### Step 4 — investigate the `transcript` 50% failure separately (P2)

Not covered by RC1–RC4. Captions fail with `yt-dlp exited with 1` from `runCaptionsOnce` (`captions.js:122`), and a manual caption fetch on `numa` *succeeded*, so this is intermittent rather than broken. It may well be collateral from RC4 (bot challenge on the caption request) — **re-measure after Step 2** before spending investigation time on it. Volume is low (20/day) so it is genuinely lower priority, but 50% is not acceptable long-term.

### Step 5 — operational hygiene (P2/P3)

- **`bgutil-pot` OOMs at the 2 GB V8 heap limit** — `FATAL ERROR: Reached heap limit`, 11 restarts on `numa`, 8 on `db`. It is up and minting tokens, so this is not currently causing failures, but `numa` has only **2 GB RAM available** with the API at 2.9 GB RSS. Consider `--max-old-space-size` on the pot process, or a scheduled restart. Watch `numa` memory generally.
- **ffmpeg skew:** `numa` 6.1.1, `db` 4.4.2. Not implicated in anything here, but the two API nodes should not differ.
- **Carried from session-033, still open:** derive `GIT_SHA` at build time; enumerate OpenObserve alerts for the `IP_LOCK_FAILED` → `MEDIA_FETCH_403` rename; fix the canary's single-datacenter-IP sampling; add `smoke-web-assets.mjs` to web deploys; widen Kuma monitor #46's interval.

### Explicitly NOT recommended

- **Upgrading yt-dlp** — already on `2026.08.19`, the latest PyPI stable. (A nightly/master build is a *possible* Step 1 alternative if `mweb` disappoints, since YouTube fixes land there first, but it trades stability for freshness and should not be a first move.)
- **Reverting the session-033 tier order to `direct` first** — `direct` is measurably the worst tier (51–71% daily failure, and 403s where the residential proxy gets 206). Session-033's flip was justified and the data still supports it; the problem is that its first choice is now empty, which is what Step 3c addresses.
- **Restarting the API to "clear" anything** — both nodes are healthy; the failures are upstream of process state.

## 6. Suggested sequencing

1. **Step 1** (`mweb`) alone, then hold ~2 hours and measure. It should be attributable on its own — this is the one change with a clean, fast signal.
2. **Step 3c** (tier reorder) next — free, and it stops burning an attempt per download.
3. **Step 2** (`curl_cffi`) after, `numa` first. Its effect shows in `BotChallengeError` rate, which is independent of the Step 1 metric, so a slight overlap in windows is tolerable.
4. **Step 3a/3b** (commercial) on the user's decision, before ~2026-09-06.
5. **Step 4** (transcripts) only after Step 2 has had a day to show its effect.

Each step is env or package level. **None requires a deploy, a build, or a migration** — which is deliberate: it keeps every change revertible in under a minute and independently attributable, which is exactly what session-033 §7 asked for and what made its own results readable.

## 8. Open questions

- **Why did `web_safari` stop working between 2026-08-17 and now?** YouTube-side, presumably, and the exact date is recoverable from the `muxed` daily series (it was already 61.5% on 08-26, and 30% on 08-17) — so the regression landed somewhere in 08-18…08-25, overlapping the spike that masked it. Worth pinning down only if it helps predict the next one.
- **Will `mweb` degrade the same way?** Probably, eventually. That is the argument for the prioritised-client-list code change noted under Step 1 rather than swapping one hardcoded client for another.
- **What cleared the 08-18→08-23 spike?** No change of ours correlates. If it was a YouTube experiment, it can come back.
- **`FormatNotFoundError` ×819 and `TypeError` ×211** were not investigated. The `TypeError` count in particular suggests a code-level defect worth a look in a later session.
